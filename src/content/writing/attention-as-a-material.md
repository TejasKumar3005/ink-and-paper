We talk about attention as if it were currency: something you spend, pay,
budget, waste. The metaphor is so settled that it is hard to see, and like
most settled metaphors it quietly decides the argument before anyone has it.

Money is fungible. An hour of your attention at nine in the morning is not the
same material as an hour at four in the afternoon, any more than green wood is
the same material as seasoned wood. You can work both. You cannot work them
the same way.

## Grain

Every material has a direction it prefers. Attention has one too, and it is
not the direction most working days are cut in.

Mine runs long and narrow. Given three uninterrupted hours I can do work that
four interrupted hours cannot approximate — not slower, *different*. The
interrupted version is a different object made by a different process, and no
amount of diligence turns one into the other.

<aside class="pullquote">
Knowing the grain does not make the work easier. It makes the failures
predictable, which is nearly as good.
</aside>

## Limit

The second thing a material teaches you is where it breaks. Paper has a fold
count. Attention has an afternoon.

I used to treat the afternoon as a personal failing to be corrected with
coffee and resolve. I now treat it as a property of the stock — the hours
where I can edit but not draft, answer but not decide, print but not sequence.
Reorganising around that was the single largest improvement I have made to my
working life, and it required no discipline at all. It required a schedule.

> You do not motivate oak into behaving like pine. You find out what oak is
> for.

## Best use

The last thing is the hardest, because it is a judgement rather than a fact:
what is this particular material *for*?

The honest answer for most of us is that a lot of our best attention goes to
things that would be perfectly well served by our worst. The work that needs
the long narrow grain is a small fraction of the week, and it is routinely
scheduled in the offcuts.

I do not have a system for this. I have a question I ask on Sunday evening,
which is: what is the one thing this week that will only be good if it gets
the good hours? Then I put it at nine in the morning and let everything else
arrange itself around it.

It works about two weeks in three, which is a better rate than anything else
I have tried.
